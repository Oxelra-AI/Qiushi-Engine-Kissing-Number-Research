# Dimension 27: reallocating a second layer

[中文](27.zh-CN.md) · [Research overview](../README.md) · [Lifting framework](../trajectory/leech.md)

The $\boxed{201567}$-point configuration keeps a 2,258-head first
layer and replaces a 310-owner second layer by a 311-owner selection.
Each additional owner contributes two caps and removes one equatorial
point. The gain is therefore one point in the complete configuration.

## What changes, and what remains fixed

The two-layer construction is developed in Kravatskiy's public package;
[Lindow's joint optimization](https://github.com/alexlegeartis/KissingNumbers/pull/1)
supplies the 201,566-point input. Its 2,090 deleting heads and 168
nondeleting heads carry three caps each. Qiushi Engine retains all these
heads, their labels, and the twelve pure-tail points.

The question becomes a finite one: with this first layer fixed, which
Leech directions can carry an antipodal pair of second-layer caps, and
how should they be distributed among three coordinate lines?

## The candidate graph

Use integer Leech coordinates of squared norm 32. Let $Y$ denote the
first-layer heads, of squared norm 192. A second-layer owner $u$ must
remain outside the first deletion set and satisfy

$$\max_{y\in Y}y\cdot u\le32.$$

Reconstructing the shell gives 3,125 candidates. Join two candidates
when $u\cdot u'>8$: these are positive dot-16 conflicts, not absolute
inner-product conflicts. The graph has 261,474 edges. A selected owner
receives one of three labels, with adjacent selected owners differently
labelled. The finite optimization is

$$\max\sum_{u,j}z_{uj},\qquad
\sum_jz_{uj}\le1,\qquad
z_{uj}+z_{u'j}\le1\quad(uu'\text{ an edge}),\quad z_{uj}\in\{0,1\}.$$

Different-label pairs already satisfy the shell bound $u\cdot u'\le16$.
The original 310-owner choice admitted no direct insertion on any label.
Joint reselection and recolouring reached 311 owners, divided as
$105+103+103$. The saved allocation is a feasible certificate for this
311-owner selection.

## Why the graph solution gives a kissing configuration

Normalize the complete construction to squared norm 4. Owner $u$ with
label $j$ produces

$$\left(\sqrt{3/32}\,u,e_j\right),\qquad
\left(\sqrt{3/32}\,u,-e_j\right).$$

The two copies have mutual product 2. Distinct owners on one line have
product at most $3\cdot8/32+1=7/4$; on different lines the bound is
$3\cdot16/32=3/2$. The first-layer constraint makes their products
with that layer at most $(\sqrt6+2\sqrt3)/3<2$.
Products with retained equatorial points are at most $\sqrt3<2$.
The pure-tail maximum is 2 and is attained.

The two deletion sets are disjoint, so

$$196560-2090-311+3\cdot2258+2\cdot311+12=\boxed{201567}.$$

## Finite inputs and search reconstruction

[Four arrays](../../constructions/d27/data/) specify the first-layer
heads and labels, and the new owners and labels. The
[independent verifier](../../tools/verify_dimension27.py) rebuilds the
shell and checks the deletions, integer thresholds and exact tail bounds.
The [candidate reconstruction](../../constructions/d27/search.py)
regenerates the graph and implements the binary optimization above.

```sh
python3 tools/reproduce.py --suite dimension27 --output ../check-d27
python3 constructions/d27/search.py --output ../d27-candidates
```

The second command reconstructs the graph and checks the saved witness;
its optional solver mode searches for larger selections. The explicit
311-owner allocation specifies the result independently of search time.
