# Dimension 32: growing a support code by joint replacement

[中文](32.zh-CN.md) · [Research overview](../README.md) · [Shared construction](../trajectory/codes.md)

The 32-dimensional result is a 347,584-point configuration. Its discovery
centred on a finite question: how can several new supports enter a code
together when none can be inserted without disturbing the existing family?

## From geometry to a search over supports

In the Edel–Rains–Sloane construction, an eight-element support carries
128 signed points. Distinct supports must intersect in at most four
positions. With the 131,072-word dense layer and the fixed root layer,
the point count is

$$N=131072+128|\mathcal B|+2\cdot32\cdot31.$$

The public comparison used 1,667 supports. Finding nine more would therefore
add 1,152 points without redesigning either of the other layers. The
geometric problem had become a packing problem for eight-subsets, but a
maximal family need not admit another support by direct insertion.

Qiushi Engine searched for replacements. For a new support $b$, write
$F(b)$ for the old supports intersecting it in more than four positions.
A compatible collection $Y$ can replace $F(Y)=\bigcup_{b\in Y}F(b)$;
its gain is $|Y|-|F(Y)|$. This union is the important quantity. Several
candidates can share the same obstruction, so paying for their removals
jointly can be profitable even when adding any one candidate is not.

## The support family that emerged

The support-exchange search produced a family of 1,676 eight-subsets.
The saved endpoint was independently rebuilt and checked: all
$\binom{1676}{2}=1,403,650$ pairs have intersection at most four.
The [complete family](../../constructions/codes/data/d32_supports_binary.txt)
is the finite object defining the result.

The research also tested whether this family contained a particularly
favourable region for a signed local replacement, as in dimensions 35–37.
The tested regions were unions of three supports with pairwise intersection
four and twelve coordinates in total. Among 14,739 such triples, the best
region had only four of its 495 eight-subsets free of outside conflicts.
This explained why the same local mechanism was not immediately productive
here: enlarging a support code and exposing a useful local region are
different objectives. The final 32-dimensional construction uses the
support improvement itself.

The [region census](../data/support-regions.json) records the input family,
enumeration rule and a maximizing region. Its
[recounting program](../../tools/check_support_regions.py) reconstructs
all 14,739 regions and the maximum of four from the published supports.

## Translating the code back into geometry

The dense layer is reconstructed from the Cheng–Sloane code, with 131,072
words and minimum Hamming distance eight. Together with the new support
family and 1,984 root points, it gives

$$131072+128\cdot1676+1984=\boxed{347584}.$$

The new finite support family is the contribution here; the three-layer
geometric framework and dense-code input retain their classical attribution.
The [construction account](../trajectory/codes.md) explains the pairwise
geometric bounds and the joint-exchange search formulation.

## What this route made possible

Dimension 32 illustrates why the search must be allowed to change membership,
not merely append points. It also supplied a useful contrast for the other
dimensions: a larger code is valuable on its own, whereas a slightly
different code may be valuable because it admits a much richer local
replacement. The combination of these two criteria becomes explicit in
[dimension 37](37.md).
