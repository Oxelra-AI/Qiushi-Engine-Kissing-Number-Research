# Dimension 45: a small fibre controls a large kissing configuration

[中文](45.zh-CN.md) · [Research overview](../README.md) · [Shared construction](../trajectory/sections.md)

The 7,380,090-point construction joins two ideas: a moment inequality
that rewards each vector in a small fibre, and a graph search that
makes that fibre easy to find. The proof and the search were designed
around the same statistic.

## Choosing a statistic with a positive contribution

For a rank-three section with Gram matrix $8I_3-2J_3$, the possible
inner-product signatures of mother-shell vectors form a finite domain.
There are 239 signatures. Eight are exceptional signatures of section
roots, each with multiplicity one. Subtracting their contributions leaves
231 signatures; pairing nonzero antipodes and retaining the zero signature
separately gives $(239-8+1)/2=116$ unknown multiplicities. The even-degree
monomials of total degree at most ten give 161 rational moment equations.

Rather than solve every fibre count, the research sought an inequality
for the desired projected configuration. The resulting certificate is

$$T\ge7377408+9f(-2,-2,3).$$

The positive coefficient nine changes the search task. Each distinct
vector with the specified signature improves the proved lower bound.
One can exhibit a good witness set without first proving that every
vector in its fibre has been found.

## Replacing repeated enumeration by one graph

Choose a norm-eight parent $p$ and its fibre

$$R(p)=\{x:x^2=6,\ p\cdot x=4\}.$$

The exact design moments show that $|R(p)|=2256$. The involution
$x\mapsto p-x$ pairs these vectors into 1,128 vertices. For suitable
choices $r,s\in R(p)$, the section $(-s,s-p,p-r)$ has the required
Gram matrix. Write $\bar r,\bar s$ for the graph vertices containing
$r,s$, and $N(\bar r),N(\bar s)$ for their neighbourhoods. Two distinct
vertices are adjacent when representatives have inner product one or
three. The target witnesses correspond to
$N(\bar r)\setminus N(\bar s)$: in each selected pair, orient the
endpoint $a$ so that $r\cdot a=3$. Then $s\cdot a=2$, and $w=p-a$
has the required signature $(-2,-2,3)$.

This lets many candidate sections share one expensive enumeration.
The search examines pairs in a 1,128-vertex graph instead of starting
a lattice enumeration for every section. In the selected graph the
degree is 368. For the selected nonadjacent pair,

$$|N(\bar r)\setminus N(\bar s)|
=368-|N(\bar r)\cap N(\bar s)|=368-70=298.$$

Thus minimizing the common-neighbour count maximizes the witness set.

## From a lower certificate to an exact identity

The section contains the norm-eight vectors $t_i+t_j$. The ambient
minimum gives $|y_i+y_j|\le4$ for every minimal-vector signature.
These inequalities remove 30 of the original signatures, leaving 201
ordinary signatures and eight exceptions. The stored rational multiplier
has zero slack on all 101 remaining ordinary antipodal orbits. Thus

$$T=7377408+9f(-2,-2,3).$$

The graph correspondence is also reversible. A vector $w$ of the target
signature necessarily satisfies $p\cdot w=4$, $r\cdot w=1$ and
$s\cdot w=2$; the endpoint $a=p-w$ recovers exactly one vertex of
$N(\bar r)\setminus N(\bar s)$. Consequently the 298 witnesses are the
complete fibre. Degree-ten design moments show that every norm-eight
anchor graph in an extremal even unimodular lattice of rank 48 is
368-regular, with 1,128 vertices. For any nonadjacent pair its section
therefore has exactly

$$7380720-9|N(\bar r)\cap N(\bar s)|$$

projected points. The specific count 7,380,090 is an application of this
general graph formula. The [rational statistics checker](../../constructions/sections/d45/verify_statistics.py)
reconstructs both signature domains, the identity and the anchor moments.

## The signed geometry of the anchor graph

Choose one endpoint $r_i$ from each pair and set $u_i=r_i-p/2$.
The signed matrix defined by $S_{ii}=0$ and $S_{ij}=u_i\cdot u_j$
for $i\ne j$ satisfies

$$S^2=88S+368I,\qquad
\operatorname{Spec}(S)=\{92^{(47)},(-4)^{(1081)}\}.$$

The off-diagonal identities force every common-neighbour count $c$ to
be even. Consequently every projected count $7380720-9c$ is divisible
by 18. These restrictions hold for every squared-norm-eight anchor
under the same lattice hypotheses. The [shared derivation](../trajectory/sections.md#the-anchor-graph-counts-the-complete-fibre)
connects the signed spectrum with the count, and the
[parent checker](../../constructions/sections/d45/verify_parent.py)
verifies the signed-frame identities for the supplied graph.

## How the search reached 298 witnesses

Earlier sections supplied 288 and then 292 witnesses. The search then
varied the parent vector itself in the ternary quadratic-residue
model of $P_{48q}$. In integer coordinates scaled by $1/\sqrt{12}$,
odd-coordinate parents of shape $(3^6,1^{42})$ have squared norm eight.
Searching these parents produced the graph with 298 witnesses.

The selected tetrahedron subsequently explained a connection between
candidate families. If $r^2=s^2=6$, $p\cdot r=p\cdot s=4$ and
$r\cdot s=2$, then both $r-s$ and $r+s-p$ have norm eight. In the
successful configuration, the former has even integer coordinates
and the latter odd coordinates; both recover the same witness count.
Thus the valuable geometric object is the tetrahedron and its edge
parents, not one privileged coordinate parity. Changing the parent
generation enlarged the search, while the final geometry related
its apparently different sources.

## The exact construction count

The complete 298-point fibre and the exact moment identity give

$$7377408+9\cdot298=\boxed{7380090}.$$

The projection argument puts the resulting vectors on squared radius
$23/4$, with pairwise inner products at most $23/8$. Normalization
therefore gives a kissing configuration. The gain over the fixed
public comparison 7,379,838 is 252 points.
The selected pair uniquely minimizes the codegree at 70 among the
428076 nonadjacent pairs of this anchor graph. Thus 7,380,090 is the
largest projected count for this fixed anchor and specified Gram,
not an optimum over other anchors, lattices or section Grams.
