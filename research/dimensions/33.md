# Dimension 33: sharing the cost of an exchange

[中文](33.zh-CN.md) · [Research overview](../README.md) · [Shared construction](../trajectory/codes.md)

The 363,968-point construction uses 1,803 weight-eight supports. The
decisive search principle is visible in its last saved exchange: ten new
supports have twenty conflicts when counted separately, but together they
require deleting only nine old supports.

## Why individual insertion misses the gain

Two supports are compatible when their intersection has size at most four.
For an incumbent family $\mathcal B$, let $F(b)$ be the incompatible old
supports of a candidate $b$. The correct replacement objective is

$$|Y|-\left|\bigcup_{b\in Y}F(b)\right|,$$

with the additional requirement that the new supports in $Y$ are mutually
compatible. It is not the sum of individual gains $1-|F(b)|$.

The support search generated candidates with small blocking sets, selected
them jointly, and rebuilt the blocking relation after each accepted
exchange. A saved intermediate family had 1,800 supports. The next bounded
search, over 1,525 candidates with at most two blockers, found no positive
exchange. This exhausted that finite selection problem, not the larger
space of possible support families. Subsequent constructions reached
1,802 and then 1,803 supports.

## A complete, inspectable final transition

The [1,802-support parent](../../constructions/codes/data/exchanges/d33_parent.txt)
and the [removed and added supports](../../constructions/codes/data/exchanges/exchanges.json)
specify the final exchange exactly. Direct intersection counts give:

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---:|
| New supports | 10 |
| Individual blocker counts | one of size 1, eight of size 2, one of size 3 |
| Sum of individual blocker counts | 20 |
| Distinct old supports removed | 9 |
| Net gain | 1 |

Every new support has an old conflict. None yields a positive gain by
itself. Yet their conflicts overlap sufficiently that the whole selection
enlarges the family. One old support is shared by four blocking sets.
This is a concrete example of the mathematical advantage of joint choice,
not merely a larger search budget.

## The resulting configuration

The final family has fifteen more supports than the 1,788-support public
comparison. With the unchanged dense and root layers,

$$131072+128\cdot1803+2\cdot33\cdot32
=\boxed{363968}.$$

The full improvement is $15\cdot128=1920$ points. The ten-for-nine exchange
is the last recorded transition, contributing 128 of those points; the
[final support list](../../constructions/codes/data/d33_supports.txt)
defines the whole construction.

## How this informed the other searches

Grouping candidates by their old conflicts made the search sensitive to
shared deletion costs. Enforcing compatibility through five-subsets then
made large candidate pools manageable: two eight-subsets conflict exactly
when they share a five-subset. [Dimension 34](34.md) shows this constraint
generation in a saved search, while [dimension 39](39.md) exhibits the same
shared-cost mechanism with a different dense layer.

The [exchange verifier](../../constructions/codes/verify_exchanges.py)
reconstructs the old-to-new transition. The
[primary](../../constructions/codes/verify.py) and
[independent](../../constructions/codes/verify_independent.py) checks verify
the finite inputs to the geometric construction.
