# Dimension 25: moving a contact layer to make room for one point

[中文](25.zh-CN.md) · [Research overview](../README.md) · [Construction and proof](../../constructions/d25/construction.tex)

The configuration has $\boxed{197580}$ points. The gain comes from a
coordinated motion of 552 equatorial points, followed by two local repairs.
The motion preserves all distances within the moving set and controls its
interaction with the untouched equator by convexity.

## From a fixed candidate pool to a geometric change

The starting point is Kravatskiy's
[197,579-point construction](https://github.com/alexlegeartis/KissingNumbers/tree/cf14c5ef4db6059bbf2e570e3f0ce24edfda243e/verifications/improved/dim25-lens-heads).
It replaces 1,016 Leech-shell points by pairs of caps, and includes two poles
and one additional equatorial point. Its 44 rational heads had already been
selected jointly from a finite candidate pool. Enlarging that particular
selection was not the only available freedom: the retained shell itself
could move.

Qiushi Engine examined the contacts around a complete 552-point block.
Write $L$ for the Leech shell of squared norm 4, choose the supplied norm-6
vector $v$, and put $n=v/\sqrt6$. Every removed point
$u\in U=\{u\in L:u\cdot v=-3\}$ has a retained partner $w=u+v$.
The two partners lie on opposite sides of $n^\perp$:

$$u=r-\sqrt{3/2}\,n,\qquad w=r+\sqrt{3/2}\,n,
\qquad r\perp n,\quad r^2=5/2.$$

Thus the 552 retained partners can move together in the plane spanned by
$n$ and the new coordinate, without changing their transverse geometry.

## A motion with an algebraic endpoint

All final points have squared norm 4 and must have mutual products at most 2.
Replace $(w,0)$ by $W(r)=(r+an,b)$, where

$$a=\frac{\sqrt3}{2}+\frac{\sqrt2}{4},\qquad
b=\frac{\sqrt6-2}{4}.$$

The equations $a^2+b^2=3/2$ and $\sqrt3a-b=2$ preserve the moving
points' norms and put all of them in contact with
$Q=(\sqrt3n,-1)$. Moreover,

$$W(r_i)\cdot W(r_j)=w_i\cdot w_j.$$

The horizontal part $r+an$ is a convex combination of $u$ and $w$.
Every unchanged equatorial point is compatible with both, so it is
compatible with their combination. The proposed addition therefore does
not create a large new shell-to-shell verification problem.

## Two repairs complete the construction

This motion does not preserve every cap contact. The upper cap indexed 984
conflicts with eight moved points, and the lower cap indexed 1008 conflicts
with $Q$. These two individual points are replaced; their opposite cap
partners stay in the configuration.

The search for repairs optimized directions against the retained points,
then replaced the numerical directions by normalized integer vectors.
The final choices are $2k/\sqrt{1007176}$ and $2\ell/\sqrt{10209}$,
with all 25 entries recorded in
[repair-points.json](../../constructions/d25/repair-points.json).
Their normalization is exact. The remaining products have strict margins,
checked with 192-bit Arb enclosures. Equality cases in the moving block
are proved symbolically.

$$197579-552-2+552+2+1=\boxed{197580}.$$

The [shared motion analysis](../trajectory/motion.md) proves that, for this
fixed added point $Q$ and coordinated-motion family, at least two cap
changes are necessary. The repairs therefore attain the minimum. More
generally, $m$ boundary half-planes reduce feasibility and minimum
nonnegative deletion cost to at most $2m+1$ circle points. A complete
extension combines this boundary test with compatible replacement points.
The [obstruction checker](../../constructions/d25/verify_motion_obstructions.py)
certifies the strict inequalities used in the necessity proof.

## Reconstruction

The [data and programs](../../constructions/d25/) specify the original
heads as exact numbers, the moving block, the two repairs, and every
coordinate formula. The baseline verifier rebuilds the Golay code and
Leech shell; the extension verifier checks all new pair types. Two
deliberately incompatible repairs are also rejected.

```sh
python3 tools/reproduce.py --suite dimension25 --output ../check-d25
```

This construction distinguishes saturation from rigidity: failure to append
a point to a fixed configuration does not prevent a structured motion from
making space. The large moving set has a short exact description, and its
interaction with the unchanged equator follows from one geometric argument.
