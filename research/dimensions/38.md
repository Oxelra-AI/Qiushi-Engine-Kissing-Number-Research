# Dimension 38: finding room in an unoccupied equator

[中文](38.zh-CN.md) · [Research overview](../README.md) · [Shared construction](../trajectory/equatorial.md)

The 591,900-point result arose by adding 288 equatorial points to a
591,612-point Leech lifting. The new directions come from a different
lattice shell; their compatibility with the entire old shell follows
from one short norm argument.

## Changing the construction being improved

The ERS route had produced 576,892 points. The larger public construction
of Kravatskiy used the full 196,560-point Leech minimal shell three times,
with triangular tail labels and 1,932 pure-tail points:

$$3\cdot196560+1932=591612.$$

Qiushi Engine examined the geometry left unused by this lifting. The
mixed points have a 24-dimensional head and a 14-dimensional tail,
while their heads are shorter than unit vectors. Could new unit
vectors be placed in the head space, with zero tail, without deleting
any existing point?

This changes the optimization problem. Instead of enlarging the support
code in the earlier ERS configuration, it asks what can be added to a
different, larger configuration.

## The next shell has the right scale

Use integer Leech coordinates in which the minimal squared norm is 32.
A mixed point has head $u/\sqrt{48}$ with $u^2=32$. A norm-48 vector
$v$ can therefore give a unit equatorial point $(v/\sqrt{48},0)$.
It needs $v\cdot u\le24$ against every mixed head.

Because both vectors belong to the same lattice and have different
norms, $v+u$ and $v-u$ are nonzero lattice vectors. Hence

$$32\le\|v\pm u\|^2=80\pm2v\cdot u,$$

so $|v\cdot u|\le24$ automatically. The entire cross-shell compatibility
problem is settled by the lattice minimum. Pure-tail points are
orthogonal to the additions.

The remaining choice is a set of norm-48 antipodal lines satisfying
$|v_i\cdot v_j|\le24$. These lines contribute two points each.

## From candidate vectors to 144 compatible lines

The search generated norm-48 vectors in the same Golay-coordinate
model as the base lifting, chose one representative per antipodal
line, and built the conflict graph: two lines conflict when their
absolute inner product exceeds 24. Compatible selections produced
142 lines and then 144, corresponding to

$$591612+2\cdot142=591896,$$
$$591612+2\cdot144=\boxed{591900}.$$

Keeping a common lattice basis is essential. Membership in two
isometric but differently represented Leech lattices would not imply
the cross-shell inequality for their stored coordinates. The public
reconstruction uses the base lifting's Golay basis for both shells.

## The mathematical gain from changing viewpoint

The old lifting uses every minimal-shell direction but does not fill
the equator. Looking outside the minimal shell reveals directions
whose normalization is exactly right for that space. A lattice norm
argument then removes all old-to-new constraints from the selection
problem, leaving only the interactions among the new lines.

The [144 lines](../../constructions/d38/added-lines.txt),
[base data](../../constructions/d38/base-data/) and
[verifier](../../tools/verify_dimension38.py) reconstruct the result.
The verifier also checks the cross-shell products directly, independently
of using the short lemma to explain them. Source attribution and the
lifting formula appear in the [full account](../trajectory/equatorial.md).
