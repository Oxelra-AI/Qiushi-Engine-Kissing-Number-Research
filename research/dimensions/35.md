# Dimension 35: from signed searches to a sharp local construction

[中文](35.zh-CN.md) · [Research overview](../README.md) · [Shared construction](../trajectory/codes.md)

The 409,676-point construction comes from replacing 128 points by 256.
The research did not obtain this by adding another complete support to
the old code. It changed what could be placed inside a carefully chosen
eleven-coordinate region.

## Leaving the complete sign bundle behind

The usual middle layer assigns a full 128-point parity bundle to each
eight-element support. Qiushi Engine first explored smaller signed
families that could overlap old supports more flexibly. One local trial
inserted 32 points at the cost of 21 old points. Antipodal completion and
larger local searches led to further gains, including 153- and 162-point
replacements for a 128-point bundle. An affine-sign construction also
gave a 192-point replacement.

These constructions revealed that the original support bundle was not
using the available local geometry efficiently. The next question was
whether that geometry could be isolated and solved as a smaller problem.

## Identifying a region with a controlled boundary

An eleven-coordinate region $W$ contains one old support. After removing
that support's bundle, every retained support meets $W$ in at most four
positions. Any signed weight-eight vector supported on $W$ is therefore
compatible with the retained middle layer. The dense and root layers
also satisfy uniform bounds.

This is an application of the free-zone replacement principle discussed
by Takhanov–Yun; its role and source are given in the
[shared account](../trajectory/codes.md). The new task was to fill this
particular region with a larger signed code, rather than repeatedly
search the entire 35-dimensional configuration.

## Transferring a different code into the region

For a coordinate pair, the Hadamard map

$$H=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad H^\mathsf TH=2I,$$

sends a vector supported in one coordinate of the pair to a vector
supported in both. Thus a signed weight-four vector transverse to a
matching becomes weight eight, while norms and inner products are both
doubled.

The source is the classical 30-block Steiner system $S(3,4,10)$.
Among its coordinate matchings, the selected matching leaves sixteen
blocks transverse. Each block admits sixteen sign choices, giving
$16\cdot16=256$ image vectors on ten coordinates; an additional zero
coordinate places them in $W$. Source intersections of at most two
give image inner products at most four.

The [constructive search](../../constructions/codes/hadamard/search.py)
rebuilds the Steiner system, examines its 945 matchings and recovers the
selected family. The [compact generators](../../constructions/codes/hadamard/data/generators.json)
give a shorter direct reconstruction.

## A construction that explains its own size

Each signed weight-eight vector on eleven coordinates extends to eight
full sign strings. Two compatible vectors cannot extend to the same
string: their supports would overlap in at least five positions, with
matching signs, giving inner product at least five. Hence

$$8|Q|\le2^{11},\qquad |Q|\le256.$$

The constructed set attains this local bound. It replaces the original
128-point bundle and gives

$$409548-128+256=\boxed{409676}.$$

The progression from partial signed families to a transferred design
produced both a better configuration and a short explanation of its
local capacity. [Dimension 36](36.md) develops the same transfer with a
different combinatorial design; [dimension 37](37.md) adapts it when the
surrounding supports impose additional restrictions.

The [finite construction](../../constructions/codes/hadamard/data/d35_construction.json)
and [integer verifier](../../constructions/codes/hadamard/verify.py)
specify the embedding and check all three geometric layers.
