# Dimension 36: a four-colour design replaces a difficult signed search

[中文](36.zh-CN.md) · [Research overview](../README.md) · [Shared construction](../trajectory/codes.md)

The 484,760-point configuration replaces three 128-point bundles by
576 points. Its final description is a small combinatorial construction:
four colours, nine supports per colour, and sixteen signs per support.
Reaching that description required changing the object being searched.

## Why the first signed model was too restrictive

Three old supports form a twelve-coordinate region. Candidate supports
near them have many intersections of size seven. For two signed
weight-eight vectors, if their supports overlap in $r$ positions and
their signs disagree at $h$ of those positions, their inner product is

$$r-2h.$$

The required bound is four. At overlap five or six, one guaranteed
disagreement suffices; at overlap seven, it takes two. A single parity
separator, useful in earlier signed constructions, therefore did not
resolve the dense interactions here. Searching more combinations within
that model did not address the missing constraint.

Removing the three old bundles exposes a stronger simplification:
every retained support meets their twelve-coordinate union in at most
four positions. The external geometry is then controlled independently
of the signs. What remains is an internal signed-code problem.

## A weight-four design supplies the answer

The research connected this problem to a classical 51-block weight-four
construction from two one-factorizations of $K_6$, used by
Kalbfleisch–Stanton and later by Takhanov–Yun. Its source is identified in
the [shared account](../trajectory/codes.md).

Split the coordinates into two sets of six. Colour the edges of each
$K_6$ with five colours, each colour a perfect matching. Taking an edge
of a given colour in each half produces nine four-subsets per colour,
or 45 cross blocks. Six further blocks lie inside the halves. The
Hadamard search examined coordinate pairings and retained 36 transverse
blocks, whose signs yield 576 vectors.

The resulting family has a simpler direct description. Choose one
colour as the coordinate pairing in both halves, and discard that
colour from the block construction. Each of the other four colours
gives nine transverse blocks. Thus

$$4\cdot9\cdot16=576.$$

Two distinct source blocks intersect in at most two coordinates. The
paired Hadamard map doubles norms and inner products, giving signed
weight-eight image vectors with inner products at most four. Within
one source block, distinct sign choices obey the same bound. This
establishes compatibility without a large case search.

## Placing the design in the full configuration

The image vectors are scaled and placed in the selected region. Their
external products are bounded by its four-coordinate intersections
with retained supports and by the uniform dense- and root-layer bounds.
Consequently,

$$484568-3\cdot128+576=\boxed{484760}.$$

The gain of 192 points is not a multiple of 128. It could not arise
solely by adding complete 128-point support bundles. The improvement
uses the freedom to choose the signed middle layer itself.

## What was learned from the search

The difficult high-overlap signed search became a problem about
transversality to a matching. The final four-colour description then
explained why a successful matching existed. Both forms are retained:
the [search program](../../constructions/codes/hadamard/search.py)
reconstructs the 51-block family and its 10,395 pairings, while the
[generators](../../constructions/codes/hadamard/data/generators.json)
and [construction file](../../constructions/codes/hadamard/data/d36_construction.json)
specify the short construction. The
[verifier](../../constructions/codes/hadamard/verify.py) checks that the
direct description reproduces the same 576-point set.
