# Dimension 34: making a large exchange search geometrically exact

[中文](34.zh-CN.md) · [Research overview](../README.md) · [Shared construction](../trajectory/codes.md)

The 34-dimensional search reached 1,960 compatible supports and a
384,196-point configuration. A saved search exposes an important part of
the discovery: a promising selection must satisfy both its interaction
with the old code and its own internal packing constraints.

## Generating candidates without fixing the replacement size

Starting from the
[1,959-support parent](../../constructions/codes/data/exchanges/d34_parent.txt),
Qiushi Engine considered 10,715 candidate eight-subsets, each with at most
three blockers in the old family. Introduce a selection variable $x_b$
for each candidate and a deletion variable $y_a$ for each affected old
support. The implication $x_b\le y_a$ for $a\in F(b)$ charges each old
support once. The objective is

$$\max\left(\sum_b x_b-\sum_a y_a\right).$$

This formulation does not require choosing the number of inserted supports
in advance. The geometric issue is to ensure that the selected candidates
are mutually compatible.

## Five-subsets expose the missing constraints

Each eight-subset contains 56 five-subsets. Two candidates have an
intersection larger than four if and only if one of those five-subsets
occurs in both. For every five-subset $Q$, the packing constraint is

$$\sum_{b\supseteq Q}x_b\le1.$$

The [search record](../data/d34-exchange-search.json) preserves the two
iterations. The search added constraints as violations appeared. Its first
relaxed selection contained 10,593 candidates and required 1,772 old
deletions, but it reused 119,476 five-subsets. It was therefore a source
of constraints, not a valid larger code. After those constraints were
inserted, the next selection contained seven compatible candidates and
required six deletions. Its exact gain was one.

The change in scale is instructive. A large relaxed objective did not
measure proximity to a construction. The useful output was the much
smaller family that survived all geometric restrictions.

## Why the seven-for-six exchange works

In the [saved exchange](../../constructions/codes/data/exchanges/exchanges.json),
one new support has three old blockers and the other six have one each.
The nine separately counted conflicts involve only six distinct old
supports. The seven new supports are pairwise compatible, so

$$1959-6+7=1960.$$

Reconstructing this transition requires only the parent and the exact
exchange list. The search observation explains how the selection arose;
the integer intersection checks establish why it works.

## From 1,960 supports to the new lower bound

The [final family](../../constructions/codes/data/d34_supports.txt),
the 131,072-word dense layer and the root layer give

$$131072+128\cdot1960+2\cdot34\cdot33
=\boxed{384196}.$$

Relative to the 1,936-support comparison, this is an increase of 24
supports, or 3,072 spherical points. The seven-for-six exchange is one
fully preserved step in that enlargement.

The reusable idea is to separate two tasks: price old conflicts through
their union, and enforce new compatibility through shared five-subsets.
The [general account](../trajectory/codes.md) develops this formulation;
the [exchange checker](../../constructions/codes/verify_exchanges.py)
and [independent verifier](../../constructions/codes/verify_independent.py)
check the resulting finite objects.
