\section{Embedded root systems and moment-certified sections}\label{sec:sections}
An embedded root system imposes restrictions on the projections of the
entire minimal shell. In the case below, these restrictions determine
the number of points perpendicular to a child section.

\begin{theorem}[$E_8$-extension count]\label{d43:thm:structure}
Let $L$ be an extremal even unimodular lattice of rank 48, and let
$\iota:\sqrt3E_8\hookrightarrow L$ be an isometric embedding.
Use the simple-root numbering in~\eqref{d43:eq:simple}, and set
\[
 U=\operatorname{span}_{\R}\{\iota(\sqrt3\alpha_1),\ldots,
                              \iota(\sqrt3\alpha_5)\}.
\]
Then exactly $2,553,792$ vectors of squared norm 6 in $L$ are
perpendicular to $U$. The same count holds for every $W(E_8)$-conjugate
of this $D_5$ subsystem inside the embedded $E_8$.
\end{theorem}

The proof uses integrality, the lattice minimum and spherical-design
moments. It does not require the Weyl group to extend to automorphisms
of $L$. We first give an explicit realization, then establish the count
under the hypotheses of the theorem.

\subsection{A realization in \texorpdfstring{$P_{48n}$}{P48n}}
Take $L=P_{48n}$, whose Gram matrix $G$ is published as CQ48a
in the Nebe--Sloane catalogue~\cite{latticecatalogue}. It is an extremal even
unimodular lattice of rank 48, minimum squared norm 6, with minimal shell
\[
X=\{x\in L:\|x\|^2=6\},\qquad |X|=52,416,000.
\]
The parent certificate~\cite{crosssections} specifies eight integer
coefficient rows $T$ and scaled coordinate rows $B$,
satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{d43:eq:embedding}
 TGT^t=H=3C(E_8)=BB^t/96.
\end{equation}
Thus the rows of $T$, interpreted in the basis of $L$, generate an actual
$\sqrt3E_8$ sublattice. The catalogue Gram and the certificate Gram agree
entry by entry. Let $T_5$ consist of the first five rows of $T$. Its Gram is
\begin{equation}\label{d43:eq:d5}
H_5=\begin{pmatrix}
6&0&-3&0&0\\0&6&0&-3&0\\-3&0&6&-3&0\\
0&-3&-3&6&-3\\0&0&0&-3&6
\end{pmatrix},\qquad \det H_5=972.
\end{equation}
This is $3C(D_5)$ in the stated numbering. Both $T$ and $T_5$ have all
nonzero Smith invariants equal to one. In particular the embedded
sublattices are primitive. If $U$ is the span of the five rows, then
$\dim U^\perp=43$.

\begin{corollary}\label{d43:thm:main}
The set $Y=X\cap U^\perp$ has $2,553,792$ elements. Consequently the ordinary
Euclidean kissing number satisfies $K(43)\ge2,553,792$.
\end{corollary}

The cardinality follows from Theorem~\ref{d43:thm:structure} and the
verified embedding~\eqref{d43:eq:embedding}. To verify the
kissing condition, distinct $x,x'\in Y$ satisfy $x-x'\in L\setminus\{0\}$,
so $12-2\ip{x}{x'}\ge6$. Hence $Y/\sqrt6$ consists of unit vectors with
pairwise inner products at most $1/2$.

The construction is specified without a list of millions of points. In
integer basis coordinates its defining set is
\begin{equation}\label{d43:eq:integer}
\mathcal Z=\{z\in\Z^{48}:zGz^t=6,\ T_5Gz^t=0\}.
\end{equation}
For any real $E$ with $EE^t=G$, the points are $zE/\sqrt6$, for
$z\in\mathcal Z$. All matrices needed for this definition are included in
the accompanying source certificate.

\subsection{The complete projection domain}
Now let $L$ be any lattice satisfying Theorem~\ref{d43:thm:structure},
and let $X$ be its squared-norm-six shell. The theta-series
identity~\eqref{eq:theta48} gives $|X|=52,416,000$.
Identify the parent section with $\sqrt3E_8$, with simple roots
$\sqrt3\alpha_1,\ldots,\sqrt3\alpha_8$. Write the orthogonal projection of
$x\in X$ onto this section as $p=\lambda/\sqrt3$.
Integrality of $L$ and self-duality of $E_8$ imply $\lambda\in E_8$. Set
\[
y_i=\ip{\lambda}{\alpha_i}=\ip{x}{\sqrt3\alpha_i},\qquad
q=\|p\|^2=\|\lambda\|^2/3=y^tH^{-1}y\le6.
\]
For an $E_8$ root $r$, if $x\ne\pm\sqrt3r$, applying the minimum bound to
$x\pm\sqrt3r$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{d43:eq:rootbound}
|\ip{\lambda}{r}|\le3.
\end{equation}
The exceptions are $\lambda=3r$. Each such projection has multiplicity one:
its squared length is already 6, so the perpendicular component vanishes.

For a transparent finite enumeration use the model
\[
E_8=\{u/2:u\in\Z^8,\ u_i\text{ all of the same parity},\
\textstyle\sum_i u_i\equiv0\pmod4\}.
\]
Its roots are the 112 vectors $\pm e_i\pm e_j$ and the 128 half-integer
vectors with entries $\pm1/2$ and an even number of minus signs.
The simple roots used here are
\begin{equation}\label{d43:eq:simple}
\begin{aligned}
\alpha_1&=(1,-1,-1,-1,-1,-1,-1,1)/2, &\alpha_2&=e_1+e_2,\\
\alpha_{i+2}&=-e_i+e_{i+1} &&(1\le i\le6).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Their Gram is precisely $H/3$.

Enumeration of $\|u\|^2\le72$ subject to~\eqref{d43:eq:rootbound} gives
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{rrrrrr}\toprule
$\|\lambda\|^2$ &0&2&4&6&8\\\midrule
Nonexceptional signatures &1&240&2,160&6,720&17,280\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
There are no other nonexceptional signatures. Including the 240 exceptions
gives 26,641 signatures in total.

The enumeration has an elementary completeness bound. Pair roots imply
that the two largest absolute coordinates of $u$ sum to at most 6.
For the half-integer roots, the largest signed sum with an even number of
minus signs is $\|u\|_1$, except when all coordinates are nonzero and the
number of negative coordinates is odd; then it is
$\|u\|_1-2\min_i|u_i|$. This quantity must be at most 12.
Since $\min_i|u_i|\le3$, the safe additional bound is $\|u\|_1\le18$.
The verifier exhausts these integer boxes and applies the parity, norm and
root bounds exactly. The norm-eight shell loses the 240 doubled roots,
leaving $17,520-240=17,280$ admissible points.

\subsection{Moments and the averaging explanation}
Venkov's theorem, as stated in Nebe~\cite[Theorem 4.1]{nebe-designs}, makes $X$ a
spherical 11-design. For $2m\le10$ and section vectors
$b_{i_1},\ldots,b_{i_{2m}}$, its moments therefore satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{d43:eq:moments}
\sum_{x\in X}\prod_{j=1}^{2m}\ip{x}{b_{i_j}}
=\frac{|X|6^m}{48\cdot50\cdots(48+2m-2)}
\sum_{P}\prod_{(a,b)\in P}H_{i_a i_b},
\end{equation}
where $P$ runs over pairings of the $2m$ labelled positions. The empty
product gives the degree-zero equation.

Average the actual projection multiplicities under the abstract Weyl group
$W(E_8)$. The nonexceptional domain has one orbit for each of its five
norms. Let their sizes be $s_j$, projected squared norms be
$q_j=0,2/3,4/3,2,8/3$, and averaged multiplicities be $a_j$.
Spherical radial moments give
\begin{equation}\label{d43:eq:radial}
\sum_j s_ja_jq_j^k=|X|6^k\frac{(4)_k}{(24)_k}-240\,6^k,
\qquad 0\le k\le5,
\end{equation}
where $(a)_k$ denotes the rising factorial. The ratio is the radial moment
of an eight-dimensional orthogonal projection of a sphere in dimension 48.
The subtraction removes the actual section roots. The equations for
$k=0,\ldots,4$ yield
\[
(a_0,a_1,a_2,a_3,a_4)
=(1,092,000,116,235,9,180,495,63/4).
\]
The equation for $k=5$ is an additional exact check.

The roots perpendicular to the first five simple roots form $A_3$.
The target subspace contains respectively $1,12,6,24,0$ admissible
signatures at the five norms, and 12 exceptional roots. Thus the average
contact count over the Weyl images of this $D_5$ child is
\begin{equation}\label{d43:eq:count}
1,092,000+12(116,235)+6(9,180)+24(495)+12=2,553,792.
\end{equation}
Every such image is an actual child inside the embedded $E_8$. At least
one child has a count at least as large as the average, proving the lower
bound. The averaging takes place within the section's Weyl group;
its transformations need not extend to automorphisms of $L$.
The $a_j$ are orbit averages of integer fibre multiplicities and can
therefore be rational, as the value $63/4$ illustrates.

\subsection{Exact count for the specified child}
To prove the stronger cardinality claim for the first five rows, average
only under the subgroup
\[
\Gamma=\langle W(D_5),W(A_3),-1\rangle.
\]
This group preserves both the full admissible domain and the target
$y_1=\cdots=y_5=0$. Consequently averaging preserves the target count,
even when the actual multiplicity function has no such symmetry.
The invariant spherical moment equations remain valid after averaging.

The domain has 43 $\Gamma$-orbits $O_j$. Write $v_j$ for the averaged
multiplicity of a signature in $O_j$. A monomial moment row has entries
\[
A_{\alpha,j}=\sum_{y\in O_j}y^\alpha,
\]
and right-hand side $b_\alpha$ given by~\eqref{d43:eq:moments}.
Actual-root orbits supply the additional equations $v_j=1$. The target is
\[
c_j=|\{y\in O_j:y_1=\cdots=y_5=0\}|.
\]
The supplied certificate selects 43 moment or fixed-root rows, and provides
a rational column vector $w$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{d43:eq:dual}
A^tw=c,\qquad w^tb=2,553,792.
\end{equation}
Since the actual averaged distribution satisfies $Av=b$, these identities
give $|X\cap U^\perp|=c^tv=w^tAv=w^tb$. The domain, the matrices $A,c$,
and the moment vector $b$ depend only on the specified root system
and the rank-48 spherical moments. Thus the identity holds for every
$L$ in Theorem~\ref{d43:thm:structure}. Precomposing $\iota$ with any
$g\in W(E_8)$ proves the assertion for the conjugate subsystems,
without extending $g$ to $L$.

The full arrays, row labels, orbit representatives and rational $w$
form the target-count certificate listed in Table~\ref{tab:certificates}.
The independent verifier reconstructs all orbit entries, all entries of
$A$, all moment right-hand sides $b$, and the target $c$ from the standard
coordinate model. Only the row selection and candidate $w$ are taken from
the certificate before checking~\eqref{d43:eq:dual}. Thus verification does
not reduce to trusting stored matrix entries.

\subsection{What accounts for the improvement}
The comparison certificate from~\cite{crosssections} records $2,545,056$
contacts. Its section is also a primitive $\sqrt3D_5$, with determinant
972, singleton cluster and parameter $\beta=0$. The old and new section
Grams are permutation-isometric. The improvement therefore concerns the
embedding of the section, not its intrinsic lattice type or determinant.

The new section is a child of a specified $E_8$ inside $P_{48n}$; the
comparison uses a $D_5$ in $P_{48p}$. The parent $E_8$ supplies additional
roots and projection relations that force the larger perpendicular shell
population. Two isometric sections can have different numbers of ambient
minimal vectors perpendicular to them. Their extension structure is
therefore relevant to contact-number optimization even when determinant
and singleton packing density agree.
