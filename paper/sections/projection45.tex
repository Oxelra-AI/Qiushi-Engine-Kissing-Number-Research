\section{A moment-certified projection in dimension 45}\label{sec:projection45}
We obtain 7380090 points by projecting three fibres of the minimal
shell of a quadratic-residue neighbour. The argument first establishes
the minimum of this lattice, then bounds the three fibre sizes by
an exact moment inequality and 298 explicit vectors.

\subsection{The specified quadratic-residue neighbour}\label{sec:qr-neighbour}
Let $C$ be the extended ternary quadratic-residue code of length 48.
We use its published parameters $[48,24,15]$~\cite{leech-sloane1971,nebe}.
The supplement constructs a generator by a degree-23 factor of
$1+x+\cdots+x^{46}$ over $\F_3$ and a self-dual extension coordinate.
The finite generator matrix fixes the factor, coordinate order and
extension.
Set
\[
M=\{z\in\Z^{48}:z\bmod3\in C\},\qquad L=M/\sqrt3,
\qquad L_0=\{z/\sqrt3\in L:z^2\equiv0\pmod6\}.
\]
An integer basis $B_0$ for $M$ and a characteristic numerator
$\chi\in M$ are supplied. They satisfy
\[
\chi_i\text{ odd for all }i,\qquad
\chi^2=264,\qquad
\chi\cdot z\equiv z^2\pmod6\quad(z\in M).
\]
The last congruence is checked on $B_0$ and extends by integrality.
Define
\[
\Lambda=L_0\cup\bigl(\chi/(2\sqrt3)+L_0\bigr).
\]
The characteristic relation and $\chi^2/12=22$ make $\Lambda$ even
and integral. The two index-two constructions give covolume one.
The supplied integer basis $B$ satisfies $BB^t=12G$ for the exact
Gram matrix $G$ of the 298-vector certificate. It is reconstructed
from $B_0$ and the two cosets by integer row operations; the verifier
checks equality with the archived $G$, not merely its determinant.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:qr-minimum}
The specified lattice $\Lambda$ has minimum squared norm six.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
In $L_0$, a putative vector of squared norm two or four has numerator
$z$ with $z^2\le12$. The minimum code weight forces $z\bmod3=0$.
Its nonzero squared norm is then $3w^2$, whose positive even values
are at least six.

In the other coset, the numerator $y=\chi+2z$ has all coordinates
odd, so $y^2/12\ge4$. Equality requires $y_i=\pm1$.
Let $s_i\in\{-1,1\}$ represent $\chi_i\bmod3$. The certificate
checks that $s\in C$ and $\chi\cdot s=66$. Multiplying coordinates
by $s$ yields an equivalent extremal ternary code containing the
all-one word. Its binary-valued words have weights $0,24,48$, by
the two-symbol specialization of the complete weight
enumerator~\cite[equation (20)]{munemasa-tamura}.
Write $y=s(1-2b)$ coordinatewise. Since $y\bmod3\in C$, the
binary word $b$ belongs to the normalized code, and its weight $w$
is one of these three values. But $\chi_i s_i\equiv1\pmod6$,
so
\[
\chi\cdot z
=\frac{\chi\cdot s-\chi^2}{2}-\sum_i\chi_i s_i b_i
\equiv-99-w\equiv3\pmod6.
\]
This contradicts $z/\sqrt3\in L_0$. Evenness excludes other
positive squared norms below six, and $\sqrt3(e_0+e_1)\in L_0$ has
squared norm six.
\end{proof}

The weight-24 theta-series calculation in~\eqref{eq:theta48}
now gives a minimal shell $X$ of size $52416000$. It is a spherical
11-design~\cite{nebe-designs}. These are consequences for this
specified Gram matrix, with the literature inputs and basis identities
stated above.

\subsection{A complete signature domain and its moments}
The three stored section rows $T$ satisfy
\[
H=TGT^t=8I_3-2J_3
=\begin{pmatrix}6&-2&-2\\-2&6&-2\\-2&-2&6\end{pmatrix},
\quad H^{-1}=(I_3+J_3)/8,\quad\det H=128.
\]
For $x\in X$, put $y_i=\ip{x}{t_i}$ and write $f(y)$ for the
number of vectors with that signature. The projection norm is
$y^tH^{-1}y\le6$. The certificate also supplies integer rows $W$
with $WGT^t=I_3$. Hence the section is primitive: if an ambient
lattice vector has the form $x=aT$, then $a=xGW^t$ is integral.
The section has eight norm-six vectors: the
three basis vectors, their sum, and their negatives. Completeness
follows because a section vector of norm six has integer coefficients
bounded in absolute value by $\sqrt{6(H^{-1})_{ii}}=\sqrt{3/2}<2$.

Unless $x$ is one of those eight vectors, the ambient minimum gives
$|y\cdot z|\le3$ for each of their section coefficient rows $z$.
The eight exceptional signatures have multiplicity one. Enumeration
over the integer box $[-6,6]^3$, with the norm and root inequalities,
therefore gives a complete domain of 239 signatures. Removing the
eight exceptions and identifying antipodes leaves 116 nonnegative
unknown fibre multiplicities, including the zero signature.

For a multi-index $\alpha$ of degree $2m\le10$, the design identity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:45moment}
\sum_{x\in X}\prod_{i=1}^3\ip{x}{t_i}^{\alpha_i}
=\frac{|X|6^m}{48\cdot50\cdots(48+2m-2)}
\sum_{P}\prod_{(a,b)\in P}H_{i_a i_b},
\end{equation}
where $P$ runs over pairings of the multiset with $\alpha_i$ copies
of $i$. Subtracting the exceptional signatures gives $Av=b$ with
161 moment rows and 116 variables. An antipodal nonzero orbit has
coefficient $2y^\alpha$; the zero orbit has coefficient $0^\alpha$.
All entries are rational and are regenerated from $H$.

Let $c$ be the coefficient vector of
\[
f(e_1)+f(e_2)+f(e_3)-9f(-2,-2,3).
\]
The rational dual certificate provides a multiplier $\lambda$, with
107 nonzero entries, satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:45dual}
c-A^t\lambda\ge0,\qquad \lambda^tb=7377408.
\end{equation}
Because $v\ge0$, this proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:45count}
f(e_1)+f(e_2)+f(e_3)\ge7377408+9f(-2,-2,3).
\end{equation}
Thus the moments bound the desired statistic even when they leave
individual fibre sizes undetermined. The checker reconstructs
the whole domain and all moment entries, then directly checks the stored
multiplier and every rational reduced cost in~\eqref{eq:45dual}.

\subsection{The witness and the kissing configuration}
The certificate supplies 298 distinct integer coefficient rows $w$ with
\[
wGw^t=6,\qquad TGw^t=(-2,-2,3)^t.
\]
Consequently $f(-2,-2,3)\ge298$. Project the shell vectors in the
three fibres as follows.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:d45}
The projections of the shell vectors with signatures $e_1,e_2,e_3$,
normalized to unit length, form a kissing configuration in dimension
45 with at least 7380090 points.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the
three fibres $e_1,e_2,e_3$ to the section's orthogonal complement.
Each projected squared norm is $6-1/4=23/4$.
Two original distinct vectors have inner product at most three.
Their projected products are therefore at most $3-1/4=11/4$ within
one fibre and $3-1/8=23/8$ in different fibres. This also excludes
collisions. Normalization gives a kissing configuration in dimension 45,
and~\eqref{eq:45count} yields
\[
K(45)\ge7377408+9(298)=7380090.
\]
The 298-vector witness and the moment inequality together establish
the count.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The change of search representation}
For a squared-norm-eight anchor $p$, define
$A_p=\{x\in X:\ip{x}{p}=4\}$. Integrality and the minimum
bound imply $|\ip{x}{p}|\le4$. Applying the eighth moment to
$P(t)=t^2(t^2-1)(t^2-4)(t^2-9)$ gives
\[
2P(4)|A_p|=90961920,\qquad P(4)=20160,\qquad |A_p|=2256.
\]
This converts completeness of a proposed parent set into a finite
check of 2256 distinct valid vectors. Relations between endpoints
can then be evaluated as common-neighbour counts on the same parent
set, replacing repeated lattice enumeration for each section.

The earlier Pless-lattice route found 292 witnesses. The 298-vector
construction instead uses an anchor in the odd-coordinate sector of
the QR neighbour: in numerator coordinates it has shape
$(3^6,1^{42})$ up to signs and permutation. Pair each $x\in A_p$ with
$p-x$. The resulting 1128 pairs are centred at $p/2$. Join two pairs
when representatives have inner product one or three; changing an
endpoint replaces the product by four minus itself, so adjacency is
well defined. The graph has degree 368, and a selected nonadjacent
pair represented by $r,s$ has 70 common neighbours.

For each of the $368-70=298$ neighbours of $r$ that are not neighbours
of $s$, orient its endpoint $a$ so that $\ip{r}{a}=3$.
Then $\ip{s}{a}=2$. With section rows $T=(-s,s-p,p-r)$, the vector
$w=p-a$ has squared norm six and signature $(-2,-2,3)$.
The parent certificate specifies the anchor, chosen endpoints and
complete parent set; its inputs are listed in Table~\ref{tab:certificates}.
The checker reconstructs this graph and verifies that the extracted
section and 298-vector set equal the final certificate.
The additional search freedom lies in the parent anchor's sector and shape.
