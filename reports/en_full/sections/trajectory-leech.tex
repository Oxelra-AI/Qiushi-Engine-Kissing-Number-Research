\subsection{From assigning blocks to reusing
directions}

The first Leech lifts retained a large fixed mother shell and used
published 496-point compatible blocks. The useful variable was the
assignment of these blocks to tail labels. In dimension 25, the latitude
at height one half permitted two poles. The count became 197,058 without
enlarging the block.

In dimension 27, twelve labels could be partitioned into four zero-sum
triangles. Replacing block multiplicities \((3,3,2,2,2)\) by
\((3,3,3,3)\) preserved one more 496-point block at the equator, giving
200,540. The capacity argument then explained the count: a direction
could occur at most three times, and equality required full labels with
multiplicity three.

The later 201,567-point result uses a different public input. Lindow's
contribution had jointly optimized the first and second layers to
201,566. The new witness keeps that first layer and increases the second
layer from 310 to 311 owners. The first layer's 2,090 deleting heads and
168 nondeleting heads remain unchanged.

New heads may contribute two or three points after their own deletion
costs, and a second-layer owner contributes one. Optimizing only a
layer's gross population can select a worse complete configuration.
