\section{Equatorial completion of a Leech-lattice lifting}
\label{sec:equatorial}

The signed replacements balance additions against deletions. Here a large
lifting leaves an unused equator. We seek directions that can be inserted
directly, compatible both with one another and with the entire lifted
configuration. A set of 144 such lines contributes 288 points, increasing
the lower bound from 591612 to 591900.

\subsection{The lifted configuration and its equatorial constraints}

Let \(\Sigma\subset\mathbb Z^{24}\) be the squared-norm-32 minimal Leech
shell, with 196560 vectors. The supplied extended Golay code generates
1104 vectors with two entries \(\pm4\); 97152 vectors with entries
\(\pm2\) on a weight-eight support and even sign parity; and 98304
vectors obtained from signs \((-1)^{c_i}\) by reversing one chosen sign
and multiplying that entry by three. The Golay generator has rank 12 and
weight enumerator \(1+759z^8+2576z^{12}+759z^{16}+z^{24}\). Distinct
vectors in this standard shell model have inner product at most 16
\cite{conway1999sphere}.

Write \(\mathbb R^{38}=\mathbb R^{24}\oplus\mathbb R^{14}\). The public
591,612-point construction uses every minimal Leech direction in a cap
layer \cite{kissingnumbers}, but leaves the equatorial subspace
available for other directions. The new construction exploits this
distinction.

Partition the 98,280 antipodal lines of \(\Sigma\) into 644 classes
whose representatives satisfy \(|u\cdot u'|\le8\) within each class. In
\(\mathbb R^{14}\) take 1932 vectors of squared norm 8 and distinct
inner products at most 4, partitioned into 644 zero-sum triangles. Each
triangle has pairwise products \(-4\). Pair a head class with a triangle
and form six points per head line:

\[
\left(\frac{\varepsilon u}{\sqrt{48}},\frac d{\sqrt{24}}\right),
\qquad \varepsilon\in\{1,-1\},
\]

with \(d\) ranging over the assigned triangle. These are unit vectors.
Equal heads with different tails give product \(32/48-4/24=1/2\);
different heads with an equal tail give at most \(8/48+8/24=1/2\).
Points from different classes give at most \(16/48+4/24=1/2\), and the
other cases give smaller bounds. The cap layer has
\(6\cdot98280=589680\) points.

Both signs on a head line are included. Opposite heads contribute $-32/48$,
so even with identical tails their total product is only $-1/3$.

Add a pure tail layer \((0,Rd/\sqrt8)\), using the same 1932 directions
and an exact rational rotation \(R=Q/D\). Its integer data satisfy

\[
QQ^{\mathsf T}=D^2I,\qquad
|d\cdot Qd'|\le\sqrt{48}\,D.
\]

The first identity preserves the pure tail products; the second bounds
cap--tail products by \(1/2\). Integer square roots suffice to check the
latter exactly. This gives the base count \(589680+1932=591612\).

\subsection{Compatible lines in the second shell}

We use the norm-48 shell of the same Leech lattice. If $\mathcal G$ is the
supplied Golay code, its integer coordinate model is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L=\left\{x\in\mathbb Z^{24}:\begin{array}{l}
x_i\equiv m\pmod2\text{ for some }m\in\{0,1\},\\
((x_i-m)/2\bmod2)_{i=1}^{24}\in\mathcal G,\\
\sum_i x_i\equiv4m\pmod8
\end{array}\right\}.
\label{eq:leech-membership}
\end{equation}
Linearity of $\mathcal G$ and its all-one word make this set a lattice.
Its minimum squared norm is 32. Indeed, for even coordinates a nonzero
Golay residue has at least eight entries of absolute value two. A zero
residue makes all coordinates multiples of four, and the sum congruence
excludes a single entry of absolute value four. For odd coordinates, a
vector of squared norm below 32 would have every entry $\pm1$; the
doubly even Golay weight then makes its coordinate sum zero modulo eight,
contrary to the last congruence. The minimal vectors described above
attain 32.

\begin{lemma}[Compatibility of the second shell]
\label{lem:second-shell}
If $v\in\mathcal L$ has squared norm 48 and $u\in\mathcal L$ has
squared norm 32, then $|v\cdot u|\le24$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The distinct norms imply $v\pm u\ne0$. Their squared norms are
$80\pm2v\cdot u\ge32$, which gives the claimed bound.
\end{proof}

Although all minimal lines are used, equatorial points can come from
directions of another length. Squared norm 48 gives the same normalization
denominator as the cap heads. The new compatibility conditions consequently
become integer-product checks with threshold 24.
The 144 supplied vectors satisfy all three congruences in
\eqref{eq:leech-membership}. It remains to check their mutual products:

\[
\|v_i\|^2=48,\qquad
|v_i\cdot v_j|\le24\quad(i\ne j),\qquad
|v_i\cdot u|\le24\quad(u\in\Sigma).
\]

\begin{proposition}[Equatorial completion]
\label{prop:equatorial}
Adding the 288 points $(\pm v_i/\sqrt{48},0)$ to the cap and pure-tail
layers gives a 591900-point kissing configuration in dimension 38.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The 288 new equatorial points \((\pm v_i/\sqrt{48},0)\) are internally
compatible. Their products with a cap are \(\pm v_i\cdot u/48\le1/2\),
and they are orthogonal to the pure tails. No base point lies on the
equator, so no deletion is needed. Thus

\[
K(38)\ge591612+2\cdot144=591900.
\]
\end{proof}

The new directions can be selected in a concrete finite graph. Filter
norm-48 candidates by the lattice congruences, retaining
one canonical representative per antipodal line. Join two representatives
when $|v\cdot v'|>24$. Each vertex of an independent set contributes
exactly two equatorial points. Filtering against the mother shell is
separate from selecting mutually compatible lines: the first calculation
is needed only once per candidate, after which the selected subset can
change without retesting the entire lifted layer. The checker also
recomputes all products with $\Sigma$, independently confirming the
consequence of Lemma~\ref{lem:second-shell} on the actual data.

There is a direct geometric explanation. Write $C=\Sigma/\sqrt{32}$
for the unit mother shell. An equatorial direction $z=v/\sqrt{48}$
must satisfy $|z\cdot c|\le\sqrt{3/8}$. This threshold is strictly
larger than $1/2$, because the head of a mixed point has length
$\sqrt{2/3}$. The space available at the equator of the lifting is
therefore larger than the space for inserting unit points directly
into the mother shell. The second-shell directions exploit this
space. Using every minimal direction need not exhaust the possible
points in the resulting 38-dimensional configuration.

The search first produced 142 compatible lines and then 144.
The completed data use the same Golay basis as the cap
construction. Canonical line representatives have positive first nonzero
coordinate and are sorted lexicographically. This convention binds the
class labels, tail triangles and added-line checks to one coordinate
system.
