\begin{abstract}
During the 2026 Apsara Conference, Zhejiang University and Alibaba Cloud
presented a 72-hour livestream of autonomous research by Qiushi Engine
on the high-dimensional kissing-number problem. The problem asks how many
equal, nonoverlapping spheres can simultaneously touch a central sphere. Constructing
larger configurations requires new contact directions that satisfy every
pairwise distance constraint. This collection presents improved lower
bounds in \ResultCount{} dimensions obtained through Qiushi Engine's
autonomous research.
The results cover dimensions 32--39, 43 and 45.

Three related construction methods emerge. In layered codes, joint support
exchanges allow compatible candidates to share deletion costs; paired
Hadamard transfer builds larger local signed codes and extends their
compatibility to the full spherical configuration. The local code in
dimension 35 attains the capacity bound of its model, while dimension 37
combines support enlargement with signed replacement. For a Leech-lattice
lifting, another shell supplies equatorial directions compatible with every
lifted point, giving $K(38)\ge591900$.

For lattice sections, spherical-design moments turn counts in a large
mother shell into finite rational linear problems. An embedded $E_8$ parent
determines the size of a $D_5$ perpendicular shell, giving
$K(43)\ge2553792$. A moment inequality for the target fibres, combined
with common-neighbour search, gives $K(45)\ge7380090$. These methods
connect geometric freedom, discrete selection and exact proof. Each of the
stated bounds has an explicit mathematical construction and reconstructible
finite data.

\par\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Keywords}\quad
Kissing number; constant-weight code; Hadamard transform; Leech lattice;
lattice section; spherical design
\end{abstract}
