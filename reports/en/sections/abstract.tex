\begin{abstract}
During the 2026 Apsara Conference, Zhejiang University and Alibaba Cloud
presented a 72-hour livestream of autonomous research by Qiushi Engine
on the high-dimensional kissing-number problem. The problem asks how many
equal, nonoverlapping spheres can simultaneously touch a central sphere.
Enlarging a configuration requires finding new geometric possibilities
within stringent distance constraints. Through sustained autonomous
investigation, Qiushi Engine obtained improved lower bounds in
\ResultCount{} dimensions.
The results cover dimensions 32--39, 43 and 45.

The discoveries share a mathematical idea: room for growth lies in how
components are combined, where they sit within a larger structure, and
which relations reveal their size. Connecting coding theory, lattice
geometry and combinatorial structures, Qiushi Engine enlarged local
configurations through joint replacements, found compatible new directions
in another lattice shell, and used moment identities to determine or bound
section sizes. In dimension 45, just 298 explicit witnesses establish a
lower bound of 7,380,090, reducing a counting problem in a shell of tens
of millions of vectors to a short exact argument.

Together, these discoveries demonstrate Qiushi Engine's capacity to pursue
an open research goal, connect ideas across mathematical fields, and
develop them into new constructions and proofs. The challenge shows how
sustained autonomous investigation can advance mathematical knowledge.
Its public setting brings this process to a wider audience, while the
open constructions, arguments and exact data allow the scientific
community to reconstruct and extend the discoveries.

\end{abstract}
